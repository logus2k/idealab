# BCG — AI at Scale & the 10-20-70 Rule

## 1. Executive Snapshot
BCG's adoption framework is anchored on the **10-20-70 rule**: success in AI is **10% algorithms, 20% technology and data, and 70% people, processes, and cultural transformation**. Their broader **AI at Scale** offering operationalizes this — closing the "AI impact gap" through process redesign, role reshape, and disciplined value tracking. The recurring published companion: BCG's annual AI adoption survey (notably the 2024 study finding 74% of companies struggle to scale value).

## 2. Context & Strategic Drivers
BCG's research consistently shows the bottleneck is **not** the model — it's organizational change. Their 2025/2026 publications stress that agentic AI raises the people/process share further, because workflows themselves must be redesigned around autonomous agents. The framework targets CEOs who have run pilots and want to industrialize.

## 3. Objectives & Target Outcomes
- Close the gap between AI experiments and real business impact (the "AI impact gap").
- Allocate transformation effort according to 10-20-70 — not the inverted reality of most enterprises.
- Industrialize a small number of high-value use cases rather than spread thin.
- Make AI ROI auditable at the C-suite level.

## 4. Adoption Roadmap / Phases
BCG's prescribed sequencing (synthesized from "AI at Scale" content and the 2025 impact-gap publications):
1. **Value-led use-case prioritization** — pick a small number of high-value processes, not many pilots.
2. **Redesign processes end-to-end** — rebuild the workflow around AI, don't retrofit AI onto legacy steps.
3. **Build the data and tech foundation** sufficient to support the chosen use cases (20%).
4. **Algorithm / model selection** — increasingly off-the-shelf foundation models plus targeted fine-tuning (10%).
5. **People and change** — role redesign, upskilling, new operating rhythms, incentives (70%).
6. **Scale and measure** — replicate proven patterns, retire the ones that don't earn out.

## 5. Training & Workforce Enablement
- BCG argues the 70% people share must include leadership reskilling, frontline AI literacy, role redesign for human-AI collaboration, and refreshed incentive plans.
- BCG runs joint reskilling programs with clients via BCG X.
- Recurring message: cultural transformation is the gating factor, not training-day count.

## 6. Product Ideation & Development
- BCG X (their build-and-design arm) co-builds AI products with clients.
- Agentic AI is positioned as the next wave of value creation — using agents to redesign whole workflows rather than augment tasks.
- "Strategic bets" alongside productivity plays: industry-reinvention propositions.

## 7. Cost Reduction Levers
- End-to-end process automation across customer service, finance, procurement, HR.
- Code modernization and software engineering productivity (BCG cites large savings here for tech-heavy clients).
- Removing duplicative manual review through human-AI handoff design.

## 8. Portfolio & Competitive Positioning
The 70% emphasis on people/process is BCG's positioning vs. peers — they frame themselves as the firm that operationalizes AI, not the one that just builds the stack. AI at Scale + BCG X bundles strategy, build, and run.

## 9. Market Expansion Plays
- Personalization, pricing, and channel optimization for share growth.
- AI-enabled M&A diligence and post-merger integration (cost and revenue synergy capture).
- New AI-native business models in regulated sectors via BCG X joint ventures.

## 10. Technology & Architecture
BCG is vendor-agnostic and partner-rich (Microsoft, AWS, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, Databricks, etc.). The framework emphasizes a modular reference architecture: data foundation → MLOps/LLMOps → orchestration → application layer → governance overlay.

## 11. Governance, Risk & Responsible AI
Responsible AI is treated as embedded in the 70% (operating model and governance), not bolted on. BCG publishes regularly on AI risk and the agentic-AI governance gap; they advocate explicit roles (e.g., a Chief AI Officer or equivalent), model risk policies, and continuous monitoring.

## 12. KPIs & Measured Outcomes
BCG's October 2024 survey: **only ~26% of companies have moved beyond pilots to generate tangible value**; ~74% struggle to scale. 2025 follow-ups frame agentic AI as the lever to close that gap. Specific client ROI figures should be drawn from BCG case-study pages and BCG X case studies.

## 13. Risks, Constraints & Open Questions
- 70% problem is the hardest to fund and the easiest to skip.
- Underinvestment in change management is the dominant failure mode.
- Agentic AI raises new questions around accountability and autonomy that most clients are not yet governance-ready for.
